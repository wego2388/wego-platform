# Sharm To Go — brand, marketing and conversion strategy

## The product promise

Sharm To Go is the traveller's local companion in Sharm El Sheikh. It helps a
visitor discover, compare and request the right experience for their interests,
party and budget, then keeps one accountable support trail through confirmation
and completion.

The customer-facing promise is not "a marketplace with many providers". It is:

> Tell us what kind of Sharm holiday you want. We will help shape it, protect
> your budget and stay available from the first question until you return.

The reusable Travel Marketplace product may retain provider and fulfilment
records internally. Those are operational facts, not the public brand story.

## Approved brand facts

The owner supplied and approved these claims on 2026-09-27:

- more than 25 years of tourism experience, with particular depth in Sharm El
  Sheikh;
- trust built with more than three million customers;
- participation in organising more than five million successful trips;
- continuous customer support, twenty-four hours a day;
- service across different traveller needs and budgets.

Before paid advertising or a regulated publication, the business owner remains
responsible for retaining evidence supporting these claims. Product copy must
not silently convert "participated in organising" into the stronger claim that
the present legal entity solely operated every historical trip.

## Naming

- Canonical brand: **Sharm To Go**.
- Canonical English line: **Sharm To Go. Where you must go.**
- `Charm To Go` is not an alternate company name. "Charm" may appear only as
  campaign wordplay, for example "Discover the charm of Sharm".

## Conversion model

Every campaign, landing page and catalog card should lead to one of two honest
actions:

1. **Book / request** — collect the service, date, party, hotel or pickup point,
   customer contact and special requests; create a durable request and public
   reference; never imply confirmation until the confirmation rule succeeds.
2. **Ask on WhatsApp** — open a prefilled message containing the same service
   context. This is a useful human channel, not a substitute for storing a real
   request once the booking backend exists.

The conversion path is:

```text
Search / social / campaign
  -> focused landing page
  -> service detail and trust proof
  -> book/request or WhatsApp enquiry
  -> confirmation summary and reference
  -> operations queue and customer follow-up
  -> completion and eligible review
```

## Public information architecture

Launch surface:

- Home — promise, discovery, proof, featured experiences and direct CTA.
- Experiences — category/search filters and published services.
- Experience detail — persuasive facts, price, duration, itinerary, pickup,
  inclusions, exclusions, cancellation, related services and sticky CTA.
- About — experience, scale, values and the local-companion story.
- FAQ — only decided operational facts.
- Contact — WhatsApp and email.
- Privacy and terms — honest current capability, updated when booking launches.

Growth surface, added by real vertical rather than one generic fake card:

- Dining: discovery and table/information enquiry.
- Accommodation: dates, rooms, occupancy and availability-aware requests.
- Car rental: dated vehicle availability, driver and delivery requirements.
- Offers: a governed promotion attached to a real service and validity window.

## Source mockups

The seven owner-supplied images are concept references only. They establish the
desired breadth (activities, dining, cafés, accommodation, cars, offers) and a
shareable booking-summary concept. They are phone screenshots containing sample

## Delivery order

1. Align the public brand, shared navigation, About/FAQ/Contact/legal pages and
   service-page marketing structure.
2. Build a real enquiry/booking-request aggregate, API and ERP queue.
3. Connect web to that flow and generate a shareable confirmation summary.
4. Connect the mobile app to the same contract.
5. Add Dining, Accommodation and Car Rental as separate vertical models whose
   invariants match the actual service, not as forced generic activities.
6. Add payment and social automation only after their security, consent,
   reconciliation and audit foundations exist.

This sequence is intentionally conversion-first: it finishes the journey from
interest to an actionable request before multiplying catalog breadth.
